## Opening (~20 seconds)

"I'm drawn to Hang Ten because you're attacking the actual enterprise software lifecycle—build, change, run—with AI, not bolting a chatbot on top. I've spent recent work making AI delivery survive real volume without turning into a services sink. Curious how you're drawing that line here, and where you need someone to own that tension."

## Five lines to land

1. **Kinage playbook story.** 0→12 institutions, cost-to-serve down 62%. The move: turn tribal knowledge into repeatable systems so you scale without hiring 10x engineers. Direct parallel to Hang Ten's bet.

2. **Bank trust and precision.** Eval gates, false-positive work, production quality bar. Enterprises will punish flashy agents that fail in the field. You care about that.

3. **TD-style judgment.** Pushback under pressure. Calm alternative, protected delivery, spoke risk and outcomes, not emotion. Shows you can hold a line with smart operators.

4. **Incomplete-info shipping.** Specs are always incomplete at founding stage. You cut scope, ship, measure adoption. No paralysis.

5. **Why this gap matters.** The companies that can use AI to change how software is built, deployed, and maintained will win. Everyone else is still bolting on chatbots. Hang Ten is attacking the real problem.

## Stories to have ready

**Kinage scale & cost-to-serve (2–3 min)**
- Situation: Multi-account AI deployment ownership; institutions had different workflows, different trust levels, different failure modes.
- Decision: Instead of custom labor per account, built playbooks. Coached deployed engineers to own delivery. Turned what looked like 12 separate projects into one repeatable system.
- System: Eval gates, cost tracking, field failure feedback into Product.
- Result: 0→12 institutions, cost-to-serve down 62%. Team leverage, not solo heroics.
- *Why it lands:* Shows you think about unit economics and scaling. Hang Ten will need this.

**Bank trust & precision (1–2 min)**
- Situation: Regulated environment. False positives are expensive. Agents that sound confident but are wrong destroy trust.
- Decision: Built eval gates upstream. Precision work before deployment.
- System: Fielded failures back into Product. Raised the bar on what "ready" meant.
- Result: Production quality bar held. Customers trusted the system.
- *Why it lands:* Enterprises like Fresenius and Siemens Energy care about this. You do too.

**TD-style pushback (1 min)**
- Situation: Smart stakeholder pushing for a delivery timeline that protected no margin for failure.
- Decision: Calm alternative. Showed the risk, the outcomes if we shipped unready, and a protected path.
- Result: Delivered on time, on quality. Stakeholder respected the honesty.
- *Why it lands:* Founding teams need people who can hold a line without ego.

**Incomplete-info story (draft if asked)**
- Situation: Spec was incomplete. Scope was fuzzy.
- Decision: Cut ruthlessly. Shipped the core. Measured adoption.
- Result: Learned what mattered. Iterated fast.
- *Why it lands:* Shows you move without perfect information.

## Questions to ask

1. **"What's the role shape you're hiring for right now—delivery lead, founding product, customer zero, something else?"** (Clarifies what they actually need. Listen for role ambiguity; that's normal at this stage.)

2. **"How are you thinking about the services-to-product transition? Where do you see the biggest risk?"** (Tests whether they've thought about the Kinage problem. Good signal if they have a clear answer.)

3. **"Who else am I meeting in the next round, and what are they testing for?"** (Leaves you with a map. Shows you're thinking about the full loop.)

4. **"What's one thing you've learned from Fresenius or Siemens Energy that changed how you think about the product?"** (Signals you care about real customer feedback, not demo theater.)

## Don't

- Overclaim domain depth in gov or MENA if asked. Say "I've seen the patterns in enterprise, but I'd learn the specifics with you."
- Ramble about AI trends or hype. Stay on the Hang Ten problem.
- Fake manager headcount. Lead by ownership and coaching, not org chart.
- Pretend the role is clear if it isn't. Embrace the ambiguity; show you can move anyway.
- Sell yourself as a solo hero. Emphasize team leverage, playbooks, systems.
