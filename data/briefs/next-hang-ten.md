Four-phase structure:

Phase 2: Solution Design & Architecture (5-10 minutes). Sketch proposed solution. What would you build, how would you use AI, what is high-level architecture. Think about where AI agents and LLMs fit. Design AI-native solutions, not traditional plus AI bolt-ons. Articulate why behind approach. Ground thinking in customer's actual needs.

Phase 3: Live Prototyping (50 minutes). Build working proof-of-concept using any coding agent (Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, Cursor, or whatever you prefer). Direct AI tool to build what you envision. Review and iterate on what it generates. Make smart tradeoffs between speed and quality. Stay focused on proving core concept. Use any languages, frameworks, or libraries you are familiar with. Validate what AI generates. Iterate based on what you learn. Explain thinking and decisions.

Phase 4: Presentation & Discussion (15-20 minutes). Walk through prototype and explain what it proves, why this approach solves customer's problem, what is still needed for production, any tradeoffs made and why. Communicate technical concepts to any audience. Understand full scope of building for production. Articulate business value, not just technical merit. Think about handing off to engineering team.

Prep guidance: Problem statement will be sent one day before scheduled interview date. Review it carefully (read 2-3 times). Think about customer's situation (what is their real pain point). Do some domain research if industry is new (spend 30-60 min learning basics). Sketch out thinking (whiteboard notes, rough architecture). Familiarize yourself with any tools/frameworks you might use. Do NOT build any code in advance. Do NOT prepare slides or formal presentations. Do NOT memorize perfect answers. This is a conversation, not a quiz.

Tech setup: Confirm stable internet connection. Test audio/video on Google Meet. Confirm screen sharing works. Make sure preferred coding agent is installed and you can use it fluently. Plan quiet environment. Have everything ready 5 minutes early.

Interview philosophy: Watching process, not polish. Rough prototype with clear thinking beats polished app that misses the point. Ask for clarification anytime. Using AI tools is expected. No single right answer. Different approaches are fine. Care about how you reason about tradeoffs. Actively engaged conversation, not passive observation.

Why they do this interview: As FDE-SE, you will be embedded with customers from day one. Translating fuzzy business problems into clear solution designs. Prototyping ideas to validate concepts. Speaking fluently to both business stakeholders and technical teams. This interview simulates that reality.
