### What they are likely testing

- Launch ownership: can you own kickoff to go-live for a customer deployment, not just advise or coordinate
- Managing technical ICs in the field: coaching, leverage, playbook building, not solo heroics
- Speed to value: their public bar is measurable impact in about two weeks; how do you cut scope, ship, measure adoption, and protect delivery quality under that pressure
- Production quality for voice agents: eval rigor, precision, false positives, human-in-the-loop design when the agent must be trusted in a customer-facing conversation
- Field-to-product feedback loop: how you surface failure modes and turn deployment pain into repeatable motion or product improvements
- People management reality check: formal headcount vs influence and ownership; be honest about what you have led directly

### Lines and stories to land

Lead with the Kinage multi-account deployment arc: 0 to 12 institutions, coached deployed engineers, built playbooks so the team could scale without you, cost-to-serve down about 62 percent because you designed for leverage. Frame each customer launch as a product launch with a clear first-win metric.

Translate production quality from regulated banking (eval gates, precision bar, field failure modes fed into Product) to voice agents that must be trusted in support or sales conversations. Show you understand the stakes when an agent misfires in front of a customer.

If Sahil probes incomplete-info shipping, use the incomplete-specs story: cut scope, ship, measure adoption, iterate. Tie it to the two-week value bar they called out publicly.

If people management comes up, be direct: you have led by ownership and coaching, not formal manager headcount at scale. Name the deployed engineers you coached and the outcomes. Do not overclaim.

Use the interview line: "The way I read the role: each customer launch is a product launch. I've been closest to that as a forward-deployed PM, owning delivery quality, coaching the work, and turning field pain into a repeatable motion. I like a hard speed-to-value bar, because it forces a clear first win metric with the customer."
