- Founder-operator fit: Do you move at his speed? Can you handle ambiguity and stay calm? Why it matters: Chief of Staff lives in the founder's calendar and headspace; misalignment is toxic.

## Stories to have ready

### Kinage: Multi-account delivery and playbook leverage

Situation: Owned delivery across 12 institutions simultaneously. Each had different specs, timelines, and technical stacks. Team was stretched, quality was slipping, and we were burning margin on rework.

Decision: Instead of hiring more people, I mapped the common patterns across all 12 accounts, built a repeatable playbook for onboarding, deployment, and handoff, and coached the team to use it consistently.

Result: Cost-to-serve dropped about 62 percent. Quality improved. Team had breathing room. We scaled without proportional headcount growth.

### Bank / regulated environment: Precision and production quality

Situation: Deployed AI into a regulated financial institution. Client had high precision requirements and zero tolerance for field failures. Early builds were failing in production.

Decision: Built eval gates upstream, tightened the quality bar before deployment, and created a feedback loop to field failure modes back into product.

Result: Went from frequent production issues to predictable, stable delivery. Client renewed and expanded. Team trusted the process.

### Incomplete-info shipping: Scope cut and measure

Situation: Spec for a new deployment was incomplete. Timeline was tight. Waiting for perfect specs meant missing the window.

Decision: Cut scope to the core use case, shipped a minimal version, and committed to measuring adoption and iterating based on what we learned.

Result: Shipped on time. Adoption was higher than expected because we'd focused on the real problem. Roadmap for phase two was clear from real usage data, not guesses.

### Judgment under pressure: Calm alternative framing

Situation: Stakeholder pushed for a delivery date that was unrealistic given the technical work. Team was stressed. Escalation felt imminent.

Decision: Didn't say no or blame the stakeholder. Instead, I framed three options: slip the date, cut scope, or accept higher risk. I showed the trade-offs for each in terms of outcomes and margin.

Result: Stakeholder chose the option that made sense. Team stayed calm. Trust went up because I'd protected them without making the stakeholder feel wrong.
