### Company model (as described)

Hang Ten is not a single-product company. Engagements vary by customer. The work is helping customers figure out:

1. What is possible
2. What needs to be done
3. The best way to solve the real problem

Problems can be generic (for example, data) or highly custom. AI/ML is a tool, not the default. Sometimes the right answer is that AI is not the right approach. That call usually happens in discovery or early stages, when the approach is still being formulated.

There is no fixed SKU and no single industry focus. Because every engagement differs, the work stays challenging. There is no repeating the same feature roadmap across customers.

### Team structure

Two related motions:

1. Customer-facing build team  
   Designs and builds production-quality solutions, deploys them, and aims for something sustainable. Works directly with customers in real environments.

2. Agentic systems / internal enablement team  
   Builds tools that map customer landscape and architecture so solution engineers and architects get a kickstart. Goal is less thrash in discovery and more time spent on the actual solution.

These connect: better internal agentic tooling should make customer delivery faster and more consistent while the company is still small.

### Definition of done (their standard)

Not enough:

- Understanding the problem
- Running an agentic process
- Producing a possible design or solution
- Building something that works only in isolation

The job ends when the solution is:

- Fully deployed
- Actively used by end users, customer teams, and/or machine-to-machine systems

Output matters downstream. Value shows up after the system exists: people using it, processes depending on it, or systems consuming it. That turns the role from clever builder into owner of adoption and real-world outcomes.

### Why the role is challenging

1. Problem quality  
   Understanding the problem well enough to recommend (and sometimes reject) AI is hard. Partial information is normal.

2. Agentic process is not the outcome  
   Using agentic coding or generation is only a means. The bar is production use and adoption.

3. Diversity of environments  
   Every customer stack, workflow, and constraint is different. No single playbook fits all.

4. Small team scaling hard  
   The team is small now and trying to roughly double over the next quarter or two. Growth pressure itself is a major challenge (ownership breadth, process maturity, mentoring while delivering).

5. End-to-end ownership  
   Own through deploy and active usage, not just design or build.

### Fit filter they were testing

They are looking for people who are comfortable and thriving in ambiguity, pace, and small-team scale-up. This is not a role for someone who wants a stable, highly structured environment.

Rough filter:

- Thrive here: strong fit
- Need stable, predictable structure: not the environment

### Tools / recommendation context (discussed)

For mid-size customers with small IT/dev teams upskilling toward agentic code generation, a sensible entry point discussed was Cursor first (with GitHub plus PR/CI gates), not jumping straight to fully autonomous coding agents. Claude Code as a later power-user path. Agents should open PRs; humans and CI decide what merges.

This fits Hang Ten’s broader philosophy: tools and agentic methods support delivery, but judgment and production outcomes matter more than tooling fashion.
