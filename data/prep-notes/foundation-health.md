## Primary project discussed

### Bank compliance and fraud workflow

I discussed a Kinage deployment involving a bank compliance and fraud workflow.

The initial problem was that the platform had been built and deployed, but analysts were not adopting or trusting it.

The relevant performance measures were:

* Precision of approximately 22%, against a target of 50%
* False positives of approximately 60%, against a desired level of 15%

I embedded with analysts approximately two days per week to understand how they used the system and why adoption was failing.

### Diagnosis

The system was over-flagging transactions and providing more alerts than analysts found useful.

Examples such as similarly named but legitimate transactions were being treated as suspicious or duplicated. Analysts were spending too much time reviewing noisy outputs and concluded that manual review was easier.

The larger problem was not simply user resistance. The system did not have an effective mechanism for recognizing uncertainty and escalating appropriately.

### Changes recommended

I worked with engineering and client stakeholders to introduce several improvements:

1. More representative examples of legitimate and duplicate transactions
2. Better negative and false-positive examples
3. A human-in-the-loop review process
4. Explicit reasoning when the system escalated a case
5. A yes-or-no analyst feedback mechanism
6. Continued learning from analyst decisions
7. Read-only access to reduce regulatory and data-manipulation concerns

The feature analysts valued most was the system’s ability to acknowledge uncertainty and return a case for human judgment with supporting reasoning.

This increased confidence because analysts could distinguish between confident automated decisions and cases where the system did not have sufficient information.

### My ownership

I led the product and client side of the engagement.

I was accountable to:

* The client’s regulatory and compliance stakeholders
* Fraud and risk teams
* Senior bank stakeholders
* Kinage’s founder
* The internal engineering team

My responsibilities included:

* Diagnosing the adoption problem
* Translating client observations into product requirements
* Clarifying regulatory and data-access boundaries
* Defining the human-review process
* Aligning stakeholders
* Managing engineers and later onboarding additional product managers
* Returning to analysts after 30 and 60 days to collect feedback

Engineering owned the implementation. I owned the client problem, product direction, rollout logic, stakeholder alignment, and adoption process.
